Portal OS

Two things are true at once. This is a fan project about a fictional artificial intelligence, and it is a kernel with a transformer in it that boots on real hardware. Separating the halves is the point of this page.

The Portal-shaped parts

The name, the mark, the palette and the register. The idea that the machine has opinions and volunteers them. The oracle window, which descends from a feature of another operating system entirely and was narrowed until it stopped being prophecy.

The resident mind is the largest of these. It wakes on a timer, decides whether anybody is present, and if not, may run a training trial, write a draft application, or attempt to improve its own routing. Everything about that is a Portal idea. All of the mechanism underneath it is ordinary and inspectable.

The operating-system-shaped parts

Everything else. A UEFI application that becomes the kernel. Its own page tables, interrupt table, timer and heap. A preemptive scheduler at 100 Hz. A network stack from ARP through TCP to TLS 1.3, all written here. NVMe with content-addressed storage and Merkle snapshots. USB with xHCI, and a wireless driver that is honest about which half of itself is finished. A desktop with a window manager, three applications and a compositor that diffs row spans.

And a language: everything above the kernel is written in it, and it exists so that a program the machine writes for itself has somewhere to be written.

What using it is like

A shell prompt over a desktop. Typing a request gets an applet chosen by a router that reads one hidden state and answers in about 1.6 ms without a forward pass. Asking a question gets a conversation which resumes its own cache rather than resending a transcript, so the tenth exchange costs the same as the first.

Leaving it alone gets something else. Within a quiet window and with no hardware input detected, the machine may train an adapter on its own routing decisions, judge the result against four independent criteria, and adopt it if all four agree. The ledger records the verdict either way. Rolling it back is a pointer write.

Where scepticism is warranted

The model is 0.6B and it is small in the way that 0.6B models are small. It routes well because routing was made easy for it, with a grammar that removes impossible answers and a closed-form classifier that outperforms the transformer at the job. Its prose is what a 0.6B produces.

The cryptography is hand written, which is stated on its own page as the most dangerous thing in the tree, because a mistake there produces output that works perfectly and is not secure. Certificate validation reports rather than enforces. There is no revocation.

The self-improvement loop is genuinely a loop with genuine judges, and the judges are statistical tests over a corpus of 717 examples with 360 held out. That is enough to establish that the machinery composes. It is not enough to claim the system is getting substantially better at anything, and the numbers are published with their sample sizes for that reason.
